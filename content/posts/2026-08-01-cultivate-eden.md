In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. As the Spirit hovered over the waters, out of the mouth of God came those familiar words, "Let … there … be … light," and there was light. God would spend the next five days creating the world that you and I have come to know. And on the sixth day, mankind was created in the very image and likeness of God.

If we read further into Genesis chapter 2, we see that God planted a garden, and Eden was to be its name — pleasure. God then took man and placed him in this garden to cultivate and keep it.

Eden, to many of us, is just a garden that God planted at the beginning of creation. But to Adam and Eve, this was the place where God would meet with man, and man with God. Where God was not known only as Creator, but as Father. A place of harmony, intimacy, and fellowship with God — a place where sin was not present, and fear was not known. This was a place of communion, and it was Adam's job to cultivate this place. He was not merely cultivating a garden in a natural sense, but was tending to a place where man had communion with his Maker.

We all know where the story goes from here. The serpent deceives Adam and Eve, and they eat of the forbidden fruit — disobeying God and no longer submitting to His word and design. In God's mercy and benevolence, He drives Adam and Eve out of the garden so that they would not partake of the tree of life and remain in that state of darkness and brokenness.

But was it God's plan for mankind to dwell in a fallen and broken world, separated from God for all of eternity? By no means. God had a plan — a plan of redemption, reconciliation, restoration. Each of these words depicts a very similar reality: bringing a person or thing back to a place of harmony and peace that they once had before.

God's plan is one where He intends to bring mankind back to that place where Adam and Eve were before they disobeyed the Lord. It may not look like a garden, but what did this garden represent? Fellowship. Communion. Peace with God. Delight. God desires that we come back to this place, and once there, we are assigned that same task Adam received — cultivate and keep it.

God has reconciled us and brought us back into a place of fellowship with Him, and He is calling us to steward that place of intimacy — that we would go into all the world pleading with them to be reconciled to Eden.
